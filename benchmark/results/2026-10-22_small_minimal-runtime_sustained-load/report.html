Sustained load to 50% degradation

Open-loop sweeps: tool calls arrive on a Poisson schedule at a fixed rate (each sleeps N(300 ms, 75 ms) in its own sandbox), and the rate steps up until delivered throughput falls below half of offered. At most 64 calls are in flight; arrivals beyond that are dropped and counted as errors. SandboxManager cells ran 60 s after a 15 s warm-up; the agent-sandbox cells (cold start, and warm pools of 32 and 50) ran 25 s after a 5 s warm-up.

How long each rate step runs

Each offered rate is one cell. Arrivals run for the warm-up plus the measured duration: 5 s + 25 s for the agent-sandbox sweeps, and 15 s + 60 s for SandboxManager. Calls still in flight then finish (the drain; nothing is cut off). The warm pool is reset before the next rate starts (the cooldown). Below saturation the drain is about a second. Once an arm falls behind, the drain can outlast the arrivals.

  • Measured windows include the drain. Delivered calls/s divides completed calls by the time from the first measured call to the last release, so at saturating rates a 25 s cell reports a 48–66 s window.
  • The long SandboxManager steps are cooldown. After the 3 and 4 calls/s cells, the harness waited 16–17 minutes for that rate's sandbox pods to finish terminating before starting the next rate.
  • A 30 s step is about as long as a 50-sandbox pool lasts. In longer runs (next panel) pool 50 was empty after ~32 s at 3 calls/s and ~27 s at 4 calls/s, because refills start slowly and average only ~2.5/s under churn. A 30 s step ends right around that point, so the sweep shows the pool at its best. At 3 calls/s the refills nearly keep up even once it's empty (94% of calls still pooled over 150 s); at 4 calls/s they don't (74%, acquire p95 17 s).

Ready sandboxes in the pool (5 s means)

Refill rate (10 s bins)

  • Refill is fast until IPs run out. On a quiet cluster the pool fills at ~13 sandboxes/s, and one 50-call burst is replaced within seconds. Under sustained churn it averages ~2.5/s, with dips every ~30 s: each deleted pod's IP is held 30 s before reuse, so new pods wait in Pending for one.
  • The pool empties fast, then runs near zero. At 3 calls/s it was empty by 32 s, but refills nearly match the arrival rate, so most calls still got a pooled sandbox. At 4 calls/s the gap is too wide and acquires fall back to cold starts.
  • Recovery takes ~30 s after the load stops: the same IP hold, playing out once more.

Reading it

  • Every arm falls below 50% at 6 calls/s, and none delivers more than ~2.3–2.8 calls/s once its pool (if any) is spent: the cluster's pod-IP turnover caps every arm that creates a sandbox per call.
  • A deeper pool holds the line longer. Pool 50 keeps pace through 3 calls/s (3.22/s delivered, acquire p95 0.34 s); pool 32 keeps pace at 2 calls/s but drops to 72% at 3; cold start is already at 68% at 2 calls/s, SandboxManager at 92%. Past that point each pool drains faster than the controller refills it, and acquires wait like a cold start (p95 17–33 s).
  • Under sustained churn a pool refills at ~2.5 sandboxes/s, the same ceiling every arm hits, so a pool buys time, not sustained throughput: pool 50 lasted ~30 s at 3–4 calls/s (see the refill panel).
  • Latency degrades long before errors appear: acquire p95 reaches 15–33 s while errors stay at zero. Errors only show at 6 calls/s, as arrivals dropped at the in-flight cap (6 for SandboxManager, 5 for agent-sandbox cold start). Only the SandboxManager cell crossed the 5% stop rule, ending ~14 s into its measurement.
  • Delivered rate is completed calls over the time from the first measured call to the last release. At low rates a 25 s cell holds only 11–31 calls, so Poisson variance pushes delivered above offered (up to 131% at 1 call/s); at high rates the window runs past the cell's duration while the backlog drains.
